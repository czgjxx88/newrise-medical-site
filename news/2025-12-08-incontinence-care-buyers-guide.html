Understanding Incontinence Care Products: A Buyer's Guide

A comprehensive overview of incontinence care options — from disposable underpads to washable bed pads — with material comparisons, cost analysis, and procurement recommendations for healthcare facilities.

Why Incontinence Care Is a Critical Procurement Category

Incontinence care products — including underpads, bed pads, and protective liners — represent one of the highest-volume consumable categories in any healthcare facility. For hospitals, nursing homes, and long-term care centers, the choice between disposable and washable products has significant implications for infection control, staffing costs, patient comfort, and overall operating budget.

With the global aging population driving a 7% annual increase in demand for incontinence products, procurement teams need a clear understanding of the options available and the trade-offs involved. This guide breaks down the key considerations to help you make informed sourcing decisions.

Product Categories in Incontinence Care

Newrise Medical's incontinence care product line covers the full spectrum of patient protection needs. Understanding each category helps procurement teams match the right product to the right care setting:

Disposable Underpads

Disposable underpads are single-use absorbent pads placed beneath patients to protect bedding and furniture from moisture. They feature a multi-layer construction: a soft non-woven top layer for patient comfort, an absorbent core (typically fluff pulp or SAP), and a waterproof PE film backing to prevent leakage.

Available in multiple sizes (45×60cm, 60×60cm, 60×90cm) and absorbency levels, disposable underpads are the go-to solution for hospitals, ICUs, and emergency departments where infection control is paramount and laundry logistics are impractical.

Washable Underpads

A washable underpad (also called a reusable incontinence pad) offers a cost-effective alternative for long-term care settings. Made from durable, machine-washable fabrics, these pads can be laundered and reused hundreds of times, significantly reducing per-use costs over the product lifecycle.

The washable underpad typically features a quilted top surface, a waterproof TPU or PE backing, and a highly absorbent inner layer. They are ideal for home care, nursing homes with on-site laundry, and facilities focused on environmental sustainability.

Incontinence Bed Pads

The broader category of incontinence bed pad products includes both disposable and washable variants, as well as specialized products like chair pads, wheelchair pads, and fitted sheet-style protectors. The key differentiator is the application scenario and required absorbency level.

Procurement Recommendations by Setting

Hospitals and Acute Care

  • Prioritize disposable underpads with SAP for maximum absorbency and infection control
  • Stock multiple sizes — 60×90cm for beds, 45×60cm for chairs and stretchers
  • Consider bulk purchasing agreements for cost savings on high-volume usage

Nursing Homes and Long-Term Care

  • A reusable incontinence pad program can reduce per-patient supply costs by 40–60%
  • Maintain a 3:1 ratio (in-use : in-laundry : in-stock) to ensure continuous availability
  • Supplement with disposable options for high-acuity residents or during laundry downtime

Home Care and Personal Use

  • A washable underpad is typically the most economical choice for home care
  • Keep a small supply of disposable underpads for travel and emergencies
  • Look for machine-washable products with TPU backing for durability

💡 Key Takeaway for Buyers

  • Hospitals: disposable underpads with SAP for infection control and efficiency
  • Nursing homes: washable underpads for cost savings, backed by disposable stock
  • Home care: washable underpads for daily use, disposables for portability
